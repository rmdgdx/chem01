IElements, Compounds, Mixtures, and Change Items 1–8

1.Classifying a substanceAnalyze

A white solid melts sharply at 801 °C, does not conduct electricity as a solid but conducts well when molten, and cannot be separated into simpler substances by filtration, distillation, or magnetism. It can be decomposed by passing electricity through the melt. The sample is best classified as

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

A sharp melting point and resistance to every physical separation method rule out mixtures (A mixture would show a melting range and could be separated physically). Decomposition by electricity — a chemical change — rules out an element, which cannot be broken into simpler substances. A pure substance that can be chemically decomposed is a compound; the conductivity pattern (only when molten) is typical of an ionic compound such as NaCl.

2.Alloys and variable compositionEvaluate

Two brass samples from different suppliers are analyzed: one is 70 % Cu / 30 % Zn, the other 60 % Cu / 40 % Zn. Both are legitimately sold as "brass." Which conclusion is best supported?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

The law of definite proportions says a compound always has the same composition by mass. Two different, equally valid compositions for "brass" show that it does not obey that law — so it is a mixture (specifically a homogeneous solid solution, or alloy). Option A invents a formula from one sample; B assumes a fixed composition that mixtures do not have; D confuses allotropes (different forms of one element, like graphite and diamond) with a two-element material.

3.Physical vs chemical changeAnalyze

A magnesium ribbon is held in a flame. It bursts into a brilliant white light and leaves a crumbly white powder that weighs more than the original ribbon. Which observation is the strongest evidence that a chemical change occurred?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Heat, light, and color change are common signs of chemical change, but each can also accompany physical changes (a heated wire glows; freezing water changes appearance). A mass increase in an open system can only be explained if the magnesium combined with something from the air — oxygen — forming a new substance, MgO. That is a direct consequence of atoms rearranging into a new compound, which is the definition of chemical change.

4.Interpreting electrolysisAnalyze

When electricity is passed through water, two gases collect in a 2 : 1 volume ratio and the water level drops. One gas burns; the other makes a glowing splint burst into flame. What does this experiment demonstrate about water?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

If water were a mixture of H2 and O2, the gases could be separated by physical means and would not require electricity; they would also be present in variable proportions. The fixed 2 : 1 ratio and the need for a chemical process (electrolysis) to obtain them are the fingerprints of a compound with definite composition — H2O. (Pure water barely conducts; a little electrolyte is added — but conductivity is not a test for elements anyway.)

5.Reading a particle diagramApply

A closed box is described at the particle level: it contains many pairs of identical red spheres bonded together (red–red) and many pairs of identical blue spheres bonded together (blue–blue), moving about randomly. No red–blue pairs exist. The box contains

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

A compound requires different kinds of atoms bonded together in one particle (red–blue). Here every molecule contains only one kind of atom, so each molecule is an element (like O2 and N2). Because two different kinds of molecules share the box without being bonded to each other, the sample is a mixture — of two elements. Air is a real example.

6.Choosing a distinguishing propertyEvaluate

A technician has two unlabeled bottles of colorless liquid, known to be pure ethanol and pure water. She may run only one test and must not destroy the samples. Which measurement will identify them and leave them unchanged?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Mass and volume are extensive properties — they depend on how much was poured, not on what the liquid is — so they identify nothing. Flammability is a chemical property: testing it burns the sample. Boiling point is an intensive physical property (ethanol 78 °C, water 100 °C): it identifies the liquid and the vapor can be condensed back unchanged.

7.Separation logicApply

A sample contains sand, table salt, and iron filings. Which sequence separates all three, and what does the success of the sequence prove about the sample?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

A magnet removes iron; water dissolves salt but not sand, so filtration removes sand; evaporation recovers the salt. Every step is physical — no new substances form — and only mixtures can be separated by physical means, so the sample is a mixture (a heterogeneous one, since the parts are visible). Distillation (A) separates liquids, not this solid mix; melting (C) and acid (D) would not separate the components and acid would chemically change them.

8.Intensive vs extensive reasoningEvaluate

A 500-g block of aluminum has a density of 2.70 g/cm³. A student cuts it into ten equal pieces and claims, "Each piece now has one-tenth the density." A classmate replies, "No — density is the same, but each piece has one-tenth the mass and one-tenth the volume." Who is right, and why?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Density = mass ÷ volume. Cutting divides both mass and volume by ten, so their ratio is unchanged: the pieces are still 2.70 g/cm³. Properties that do not depend on sample size are intensive (density, melting point, color); those that do are extensive (mass, volume). Cutting is a physical change, so option D is wrong on two counts.

IIAtomic Structure and Isotopes Items 9–16

9.Testing the plum-pudding modelAnalyze

Suppose Thomson's model — positive charge spread evenly through the whole atom, with electrons embedded in it — were correct. What would Rutherford have observed when α particles hit the gold foil?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

A thin, evenly spread positive "pudding" has no concentrated charge or mass anywhere, so it could only nudge a fast, heavy α particle slightly; large-angle scattering would be impossible. Rutherford did see most particles pass through — but the rare ones that bounced back could only be explained by a tiny, dense, positive nucleus. This item tests whether you can predict a model's consequences, which is how models are actually tested.

10.What defines an elementApply

Four particles are described: (P) 6 protons, 6 neutrons, 6 electrons; (Q) 6 protons, 8 neutrons, 6 electrons; (R) 7 protons, 7 neutrons, 7 electrons; (S) 6 protons, 6 neutrons, 4 electrons. Which particles are atoms or ions of the same element?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

An element is defined by its proton count (atomic number). P, Q, and S all have 6 protons, so all are carbon: P is carbon-12, Q is carbon-14 (an isotope — different neutrons), and S is a C2+ ion (fewer electrons). Neither neutron number nor electron number changes the element. R has 7 protons and is nitrogen.

11.Building a nuclear symbolApply

A neutral atom has 12 electrons and a mass number of 25. Its nuclear symbol is

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

In a neutral atom, electrons = protons, so Z = 12, which is magnesium. The mass number A = 25 is written as the superscript, atomic number as the subscript: 2512Mg, with 25 − 12 = 13 neutrons. Option A confuses the neutron count (13) with Z; C swaps A and Z; D adds neutrons to the mass number twice.

12.Why atomic masses are not whole numbersAnalyze

The periodic table lists chlorine as 35.45, yet no chlorine atom has a mass of 35.45 amu — every atom is either about 35 or about 37 amu. The best explanation is that

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Natural chlorine is ~76 % 35Cl and ~24 % 37Cl. The tabulated atomic mass is Σ(abundance × isotopic mass) = 0.76(34.97) + 0.24(36.97) ≈ 35.45. It is an average over a population, not the mass of any single atom. Mass numbers are always integers (A); nuclei do not swap neutrons in normal matter (C); electrons contribute only ~0.0005 amu each (D).

13.Same mass number, different elementEvaluate

Potassium-40 and calcium-40 both have mass number 40. A student concludes they are isotopes of each other. Evaluate the claim.

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Isotopes share an atomic number (same element) and differ in neutron number, hence in mass number. K-40 (19 p, 21 n) and Ca-40 (20 p, 20 n) share neither Z nor neutron count; they are isobars — different elements with equal A. Option D reverses the definition; two atoms with the same A can only be isotopes if they are the same element, which requires different Z — impossible, so D's reasoning is confused, not its verdict.

14.Identifying an element from isotope dataAnalyze

A mass spectrometer shows an element has two isotopes: mass 78.9 amu (50.7 %) and mass 80.9 amu (49.3 %). Using a periodic table, the element is

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

With nearly equal abundances, the average lies almost midway: 0.507(78.9) + 0.493(80.9) ≈ 79.9 amu. That matches bromine. Selenium (A) is close but would require the lighter isotope to dominate strongly; krypton and rubidium are far too heavy. This is the reverse of the usual "compute the average" exercise — it asks you to use the average to identify the element.

15.Predicting neutron count from the tableApply

Which statement about the most common isotope of iron, 56Fe, is correct?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Iron's atomic number is 26 (from the periodic table), so 26 protons. Neutrons = A − Z = 56 − 26 = 30. A neutral atom has electrons equal to protons: 26. The other options misassign the mass number as a proton or neutron count, or break the neutrality condition.

16.Scale of the atomEvaluate

A nucleus is roughly 1/10 000 the diameter of its atom. A student says, "So the nucleus occupies about 1/10 000 of the atom's volume, and the electrons fill the rest." Which correction is needed?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Volume scales with the cube of diameter: (1/10 000)³ = 10−12. The nucleus is thus a trillionth of the atom's volume yet holds ~99.97 % of its mass — which is exactly why α particles mostly pass through (Rutherford). Electrons are tiny and spread as probability clouds; they define the atom's size but do not fill it like a liquid. Mass concentration (C) says nothing about volume.

IIIIons and Chemical Formulas Items 17–23

17.Predicting the ionApply

Element X has 19 protons. In a compound with chlorine, X is most likely present as

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Z = 19 is potassium, Group 1, configuration 2·8·8·1. Losing one electron leaves the stable 2·8·8 (argon) arrangement; gaining seven (A) or losing more than one (C, D) would cost far more energy. Group number, not period, predicts the charge of main-group ions.

18.Ion from particle countsApply

A particle has 16 protons, 16 neutrons, and 18 electrons. It is

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Sixteen protons make it sulfur, regardless of electron count. Eighteen electrons means two more negative charges than positive: charge = 16 − 18 = −2, so S2−. It has the same electron count as argon (isoelectronic) but is not argon (A) and cannot be an isotope of a different element (C).

19.Writing a formula from chargesApply

The compound formed between Al3+ and sulfate, SO42−, has the formula

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Total positive charge must equal total negative charge. The least common multiple of 3 and 2 is 6: two Al3+ (+6) balance three SO42− (−6). Parentheses are required because the polyatomic ion appears more than once. Option D swaps the subscripts — a common error that gives +9 vs −4.

20.Inferring a metal's chargeAnalyze

An unknown metal M forms the compounds MCl2 and M(NO3)2. The formula of its oxide is most likely

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Chloride is −1, so two Cl− imply M2+; nitrate is −1, and two NO3− confirm M2+. Oxide is O2−, so one M2+ balances one O2−: MO (like CaO or MgO). MO2 would need M4+; M2O needs M+; M2O3 needs M3+.

21.Ionic vs molecular bondingAnalyze

Which pair of elements is most likely to form an ionic compound, and why?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Ionic compounds arise from electron transfer, which needs an electron donor (a metal, low ionization energy) and an electron acceptor (a nonmetal, high electron affinity). Two nonmetals (A, C) share electrons and form molecular compounds. Noble gases (D) have no tendency to gain or lose electrons and form essentially no compounds.

22.Why noble gases stay neutralEvaluate

Sodium (2·8·1) forms Na+ easily, and chlorine (2·8·7) forms Cl− easily, but neon (2·8) forms no ions at all. The best explanation is

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Ion formation is driven by reaching a filled outer shell (an octet). Sodium loses one electron to reach 2·8; chlorine gains one to reach 2·8·8. Neon is already at 2·8; any change would break that stable arrangement, so there is no energetic payoff. Physical state (C) is irrelevant — chlorine is also a gas and forms ions readily.

23.Formula units vs moleculesEvaluate

A student writes: "Table salt is made of NaCl molecules, each containing one sodium atom bonded to one chlorine atom." What is the most accurate correction?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Ionic compounds do not exist as separate molecules. Each Na+ in solid NaCl is surrounded by six Cl− and vice versa in an extended three-dimensional lattice. "NaCl" is the simplest whole-number ratio (empirical formula / formula unit). Option D describes the elements before reaction, not the compound; C invents a molecule that does not exist.

IVBasic Nomenclature Items 24–30

24.When a Roman numeral is requiredAnalyze

Which compound must include a Roman numeral in its name, and why?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Roman numerals exist to remove ambiguity for metals with variable charge. Calcium is always +2, sodium always +1, aluminum always +3 — the name already implies the formula, so no numeral is used (calcium chloride, sodium chloride, aluminum chloride). Iron can be +2 or +3, so "iron chloride" would be ambiguous: FeCl3 is iron(III) chloride, FeCl2 iron(II) chloride.

25.Prefixes: molecular vs ionicAnalyze

N2O4 is named dinitrogen tetroxide, but CaCl2 is calcium chloride, not "calcium dichloride." Why the difference?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Nitrogen and oxygen form NO, NO2, N2O, N2O3, N2O4, N2O5 — only prefixes can tell them apart. For an ionic compound, Ca2+ and Cl− can combine in only one neutral ratio, 1 : 2, so "calcium chloride" is already unambiguous. The naming system is not arbitrary: it uses exactly as much information as needed.

26.Formula from a nameApply

The formula of copper(I) oxide is

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Copper(I) means Cu+; oxide is O2−. Two Cu+ balance one O2−: Cu2O (the red mineral cuprite). CuO is copper(II) oxide (black). Option D is not reduced to the simplest ratio and would not be written for an ionic compound.

27.The -ous / -ic acid patternAnalyze

HNO3 is nitric acid. By the same rule, HNO2 is named

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Oxoacid names come from the anion: nitrate (NO3−) → nitric acid; nitrite (NO2−, one fewer oxygen) → nitrous acid. The prefix "hydro-" (A) is reserved for binary acids with no oxygen (HCl → hydrochloric). Recognizing the pattern lets you name any -ate/-ite pair: sulfate/sulfuric, sulfite/sulfurous; chlorate/chloric, chlorite/chlorous.

28.Spotting the one correct nameEvaluate

Which name–formula pair is correct?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

NaHCO3 contains the hydrogen carbonate (bicarbonate) ion HCO3−: sodium hydrogen carbonate — correct. K2SO3 contains sulfite (SO32−), so it is potassium sulfite, not sulfate. In FeO, one O2− means Fe2+: iron(II) oxide. PCl5 is molecular and needs prefixes: phosphorus pentachloride.

29.Formula with a polyatomic cationApply

The formula of ammonium phosphate is

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Ammonium is NH4+; phosphate is PO43−. Three +1 ions balance one −3 ion, and the polyatomic cation is kept intact in parentheses. Option D collapses the atoms into a single formula, hiding the ions and making it unreadable; A ignores charge balance; C reverses the ratio.

30.Reading a hazard labelEvaluate

A drum at a water-treatment plant is labelled calcium hypochlorite, a disinfectant. A trainee writes its formula as CaClO. Which is the correct formula, and what was the trainee's error?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Hypochlorite is OCl− (chlorine +1, the least-oxygen member of the chlorine oxoanion series). Calcium is always +2, so two OCl− are needed: Ca(OCl)2. CaClO3 (C) would be wrong on two counts — chlorate is a different ion, and the charges would not balance. Option D confuses two different anions; chloride does not disinfect. On a real label, that mistake could mean dosing the wrong chemical.
