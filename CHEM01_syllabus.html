Course Description

This course introduces the chemistry ideas that engineers use to understand energy, materials, fuels, the environment, and chemical safety. Lessons begin with familiar Grade 12 chemistry concepts and then move toward engineering applications through worked examples, short investigations, simulations, calculations, and evidence-based activities.

Student Learning PromiseYou will not be asked only to memorize. You will explain ideas in your own words, solve problems step by step, interpret real or simulated data, and use chemical evidence to make practical engineering decisions.

How to Use This Syllabus

  1. Before class: preview the key terms or short video and answer the readiness question.
  2. During class: participate in a mini-lesson, worked example, guided practice, and application task.
  3. After class: complete a short check for understanding and update the e-portfolio.
  4. Before practical or simulation work: read the procedure, safety notes, and data requirements.
  5. After practical or simulation work: organize the data, explain the result using evidence, and submit the required output.

Course Outcomes

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

CO1 · ENERGY

Explain chemical energy systems

Explain how chemical reactions produce, store, or transform energy, including electrochemical and nuclear processes.

CO2 · MATERIALS

Connect structure with performance

Relate atomic structure, bonding, and material structure to the properties and uses of engineering materials.

CO3 · ENVIRONMENT

Evaluate environmental processes

Explain chemical processes in air, water, and soil and evaluate basic pollution-prevention and control approaches.

CO4 · APPLICATION

Solve engineering-context problems

Apply chemistry concepts, calculations, evidence, and safety practices to a selected engineering field.

Learning Supports and Success Habits

  • Each unit begins with essential terms, symbols, and a guiding question.
  • Calculations follow the Given–Required–Equation–Solution–Answer format.
  • Particle diagrams, reaction maps, graphs, and labeled set-ups support visual learning.
  • Recordings, simulations, practice sets, and consultation provide flexible review.
  • Students cite sources, show their own solution process, and report actual data.

Flexible and Digital Learning Policies

Academic Honesty and Responsible AI Use

Students must submit their own work and acknowledge sources. Misconduct includes copying or sharing answers, using unapproved materials, impersonation, inventing or changing laboratory data, submitting another person's work, or using digital/AI tools in ways not permitted by the instructor. When a tool is allowed, the student remains responsible for checking the science, showing the solution process, and declaring how the tool was used.

Submission and Communication

  • Use the approved LMS or submission channel.
  • Follow announced file names, formats, and deadlines.
  • Report access, health, or connectivity problems early.
  • Use respectful language in discussions and peer review.

Laboratory Safety

  • Pass the orientation and pre-lab check.
  • Wear required PPE and follow instructions.
  • Read labels and SDS information.
  • Report spills, injuries, breakage, and unsafe conditions immediately.
  • Use only approved waste streams.
